Health & genetics

What health testing do the parents have?
All of our dogs are AKC registered with completed genetic testing. Many have had hips and elbows evaluated by the OFA, are cleared by parentage, or are currently under evaluation. Genetics and pedigrees are available on request.
What does genetic testing check for?
A Labrador genetic panel screens for inherited conditions such as exercise-induced collapse (EIC), centronuclear myopathy (CNM), progressive retinal atrophy (PRA-prcd) and others. It also confirms coat color genetics.
What does OFA hips and elbows mean?
The Orthopedic Foundation for Animals reviews X-rays of a dog's hips and elbows and rates them. It's the standard way to reduce the risk of dysplasia in a breeding program.
What does cleared by parentage mean?
When both of a dog's parents are tested clear for a genetic condition, the dog is clear by parentage and can't carry it. It's a recognized way to confirm genetic health.
Can I see the parents' test results?
Yes. Genetics and pedigrees are available on request, and several of our girls have their Embark results linked on the Our Dogs page.
Are your puppies seen by a vet?

What shots will my puppy have?
Your puppy will have their first set of shots before going home. Your vet will continue the puppy vaccine series over the following weeks.
Is my puppy microchipped?
Yes, every puppy is microchipped before going home. Remember to register the chip in your name so your pup can always find their way back to you.
Do you offer a health guarantee?
Call or text us and we'll walk you through exactly what's covered for your puppy. We stay a phone call away for the rest of your dog's life.
Are English Labs healthy dogs?
Labradors are a sturdy breed, and health testing the parents is the best way to stack the odds in your favor. Keeping your Lab lean and active matters just as much once they're home.

Life with your new puppy

How do I prepare my home for a puppy?
Set up a crate, pick up anything chewable, block off stairs and secure cords and trash cans. Our puppy guides include a full checklist.
Are your puppies crate trained?

What food should I feed my puppy?
Keep your puppy on the food we've been using for the first couple of weeks to avoid an upset stomach. Ask us at pickup and we'll share exactly what they've been eating.
How much exercise does a Lab puppy need?
Short play sessions and gentle walks are plenty while their joints are growing. Avoid long runs and repetitive jumping until your vet says their growth plates have closed.
When should I start training?
Right away. Labs are eager to please and learn quickly, so start with their name, sit and potty routines in the first week.
How do I potty train a Lab puppy?
Take your puppy out after every meal, nap and play session, and praise them the moment they go. Consistency and a regular schedule do most of the work.
Do Labs shed a lot?
Yes. English Labs have a thick double coat and shed year-round, more heavily in spring and fall. Regular brushing keeps it under control.
How long do Labradors live?
Most Labradors live around 10 to 12 years. Keeping them at a healthy weight is one of the best ways to add good years.
Can I call you after I bring my puppy home?
Please do. We stay a phone call away for the rest of your dog's life and love hearing how our pups are doing.
How do I help my puppy through the first night?
Place the crate near your bed, use the blanket that smells like their litter, and expect a few wake-ups. Calm reassurance and a quick potty break usually settle them.
Are Labs good for first-time dog owners?
Yes. Labs are friendly, forgiving and easy to train, which makes them one of the best breeds for first-time owners.
Are Labs good with other pets?
Generally, yes. Labs are social dogs, and introducing them to other pets calmly and early helps them settle in.
Do Labs need a fenced yard?
A fence helps, but it isn't required. Plenty of Labs thrive with daily walks, play and a good routine.
How much does a Lab cost to keep each year?
Expect to budget for quality food, vet visits, preventatives, grooming supplies and toys. Your vet can help you plan for your area.
When should I spay or neuter my Lab?
Talk to your vet. Many now recommend waiting until a large-breed dog has finished growing.
How do I socialize my Lab puppy?

Should I get puppy insurance?

How often should I groom my Lab?
Brush your Lab once or twice a week, and more during shedding season. Bathe only when needed, and keep nails trimmed and ears clean and dry.
Can my Lab puppy swim?
Most Labs love water, but introduce it slowly in calm, shallow spots. Always supervise swimming and rinse off chlorine or lake water afterward.

Training & behavior

When should I start training my Lab puppy?
The day you bring them home. Eight-week-old puppies can already learn their name, sit and where to potty. Keep sessions to a few minutes, use treats and praise, and stay consistent. See our crate and potty training guide.
How do I stop my Lab puppy from biting?
Mouthing is normal puppy behavior. Redirect to a chew toy, calmly pause play when teeth touch skin, and give plenty of naps and exercise. Most Labs outgrow heavy mouthing by five or six months with consistent redirection.
Are Labradors easy to train?
Yes. Labs are food-motivated, eager to please and quick learners, which is why they're so common as service and working dogs. They do best with positive, consistent training and enough daily activity.
How long does it take to potty train a Lab puppy?
Most puppies are mostly reliable in two to four months, though accidents can happen up to six months. Frequent trips outside, a fixed schedule and a crate make it much faster.
Should I crate train my Lab puppy?
We recommend it. A crate gives your puppy a safe, quiet den, speeds up potty training and keeps them out of trouble when you can't supervise. Introduce it gradually with treats and meals.
How long can a Lab puppy stay in a crate?
As a rough guide, about one hour per month of age during the day, up to a few hours for older pups. Puppies under four months need nighttime potty breaks too.
Why does my Lab puppy chew everything?
Chewing is how puppies explore and soothe teething. Offer a variety of safe chew toys, rotate them, puppy-proof the house and supervise. A bored or under-exercised Lab will chew more.
Do Labs have separation anxiety?
Labs are social and can struggle when left alone for long hours, especially as puppies. Build up alone time slowly, provide exercise before you leave, and consider a midday visit or dog walker.
When do Labs calm down?
Most English Labs begin to mellow between two and three years old. Puppyhood and adolescence are energetic for every Lab, but consistent exercise and training help a great deal.
Why does my Lab jump on people?
Jumping is a greeting. Teach 'sit' for attention, turn away when they jump and reward all four paws on the floor. Ask visitors to do the same so the rule is consistent.
Do Labs bark a lot?
Not usually. Labs tend to be moderate barkers who alert you to visitors but aren't known for constant noise. Boredom and loneliness are the usual causes of excess barking.
How do I teach my Lab to walk on a leash?
Start indoors with a light leash and treats. Reward your puppy for staying beside you, stop moving when they pull and walk again when the leash relaxes. Short, frequent sessions work best.
Should I take my puppy to a training class?
Yes, once your vet clears them after the right vaccines. A puppy class builds socialization and basic manners, and teaches you how to train as well.
Can I train my Lab to be off-leash?
Many Labs can, but reliable recall takes months of practice. Use a long line first, make coming when called always rewarding, and only go off-leash in safe, legal areas.

Feeding & nutrition

What should I feed my Lab puppy?
A high-quality puppy food, ideally formulated for large-breed puppies, which controls calcium and growth rate. We'll tell you what your puppy has been eating so you can keep things steady. More in our feeding guide.
How often should I feed a Lab puppy?
Three to four small meals a day until about four months, then three until around six months, then two meals daily. Feeding at set times also helps with potty training.
How much should a Lab puppy eat?
It depends on the food, age and size. Start with the amount on the bag, then adjust to keep your puppy lean with ribs you can feel but not see. Your vet can help fine-tune it.
When do I switch from puppy to adult food?
Large breeds like Labs usually move to adult food between 12 and 18 months. Switch gradually over a week to avoid an upset stomach.
Why are Labs always hungry?
Many Labs are very food-driven, and research has linked a common gene variant to constant appetite in the breed. Measure meals, limit treats and keep your Lab lean to protect their joints.
Can Labs be overweight?
Yes, easily. Extra weight stresses hips and elbows and shortens lifespan. Measure food, count treats and use part of their meal for training.
Are treats okay for puppies?
Yes, in moderation. Use small, soft training treats and keep treats under about ten percent of daily calories. Avoid anything with xylitol, grapes, raisins, chocolate or onions.
Should I feed grain-free food?
Ask your vet. Grain-free is not necessary for most Labs, and some grain-free diets have been under review for links to heart disease. A balanced, complete diet from a reputable brand is the safest bet.
Should I change my puppy's food right away?
No. Keep the same food for the first week or two, and make any change gradually over seven to ten days to prevent diarrhea.
Should I use a slow feeder bowl?
It can help Labs who gulp their food, and it makes mealtime last longer. Many Labs eat very fast, so a slow feeder or puzzle toy is a good habit.
Is it okay to give a Lab table scraps?
We recommend avoiding them. Scraps encourage begging, add calories and some human foods are toxic to dogs. Plain cooked vegetables are a safer treat.
How much water does a Lab puppy need?
Fresh water should always be available. Puppies drink a lot, so you may pick up the bowl in the evening to help with overnight potty breaks, as your vet advises.

Grooming & shedding

Do Labradors shed a lot?
Yes. Labs have a dense double coat and shed year-round, with heavier shedding in spring and fall. Regular brushing keeps it manageable. See our shedding and grooming guide.
Are Labradors hypoallergenic?
No. Labs shed and produce dander, so they're not a good choice for people with significant dog allergies. Spend time with Labs before you commit.
How often should I brush my Lab?
Once or twice a week most of the year, and daily during heavy shedding seasons. A rubber curry brush or undercoat rake works well.
How often should I bathe my Lab?
Every one to three months, or when they're dirty or smelly. Over-bathing strips natural oils from their coat and skin.
Should I shave my Lab in summer?
No. A Lab's double coat helps insulate against both heat and cold, and shaving can damage the coat and increase sunburn risk. Brush out the undercoat and provide shade and water.
How do I trim a Lab puppy's nails?
Start handling paws in the first week, then trim small amounts every couple of weeks with a puppy-size clipper or grinder. Reward calm behavior so it stays easy.
How do I clean a Lab's ears?
Labs love water and can get ear infections. Check ears weekly, wipe the outer ear with a vet-approved cleaner and dry them after swimming. Redness, smell or head-shaking is a reason to call your vet.
Should I brush my Lab's teeth?
Yes, a few times a week if possible. Start early with a puppy toothbrush and dog toothpaste. Dental health affects your dog's overall health.

Puppy health & vet care

When should my puppy see a vet?
Within the first few days of coming home. Bring the vaccine and deworming records we send with your puppy. Our vaccine and vet schedule guide lays out the first year.
What vaccines does a Lab puppy need?
Core vaccines include distemper, parvovirus, adenovirus and rabies, given in a series through about 16 weeks. Your vet may also recommend lifestyle vaccines. Follow your vet's schedule.
When can my puppy go outside in public?
Until the vaccine series is complete, avoid dog parks, pet stores and places where unknown dogs have been. Your own yard and safe, controlled socialization are fine. Ask your vet for specifics.
Do you recommend puppy insurance?
It's a personal choice, but many owners value it. Labs can swallow things they shouldn't and may need orthopedic care later, so a plan or a savings fund can ease surprises.
What is hip dysplasia?
Hip dysplasia is a developmental condition where the hip joint doesn't fit properly. It's influenced by genetics, weight and growth. Choosing a breeder who tests parents, and keeping your Lab lean, lowers risk. See our health testing guide.
What is EIC in Labradors?
Exercise-induced collapse is an inherited condition that can cause weakness or collapse after intense exercise. A DNA test shows whether a dog is clear, a carrier or affected.
When should I spay or neuter my Lab?
Many vets now suggest waiting until a Lab is at least a year old, sometimes longer, to let joints mature, but the right time varies. Talk it over with your vet.
How do I know if my puppy is sick?
Watch for lethargy, vomiting, diarrhea, not eating, coughing or trouble breathing. Puppies can decline quickly, so call your vet if something seems wrong.
Can I give my puppy human medications?
Never without asking a vet. Common human painkillers can be toxic to dogs, and dosing is very different.
Does my Lab need flea, tick and heartworm prevention?
Yes. In North Carolina, fleas, ticks and mosquitoes are active much of the year, so year-round prevention is recommended. Your vet will choose products for your puppy's weight and age.
What is bloat and should I worry?
Bloat, or GDV, is a life-threatening stomach twist seen more in deep-chested dogs. Feeding smaller meals, avoiding hard exercise right after eating and knowing the signs (swollen belly, retching) all help. It's an emergency.
How do I keep my Lab's joints healthy?
Keep them lean, avoid repetitive jumping and long runs while growing, offer traction on slick floors and feed a balanced diet. Ask your vet whether supplements are right for your dog.
Do Labs have a lot of ear infections?
They can, since many Labs swim and have floppy ears. Dry ears after water, clean regularly and see your vet if you notice odor or discharge.
What do I do if my puppy swallows something?
Call your vet or an emergency animal hospital right away, before symptoms appear. Don't induce vomiting unless a vet tells you to.

Choosing the right Lab

Is an English Lab right for me?
If you want a friendly, steady companion and can offer daily exercise, training and some shedding, yes. Read Are English Labs good family dogs? to see whether it fits your household.
How is an English Lab different from an American Lab?
English Labs are typically stockier, with a broader head and a calmer temperament; American Labs are usually leaner and higher energy. Both are true Labradors. Full comparison here.
Should I get a male or female Lab?
Both make wonderful pets. Males are often larger and a bit goofier, females a little smaller and sometimes quicker to mature. Personality varies far more by individual. See our male vs. female guide.
Which Lab color is best?
No color is healthier or smarter than another. Choose the color you love and the temperament and health testing you trust. Colors explained.
Are chocolate Labs different from yellow or black?
Not in temperament. Color is simply coat genetics. Reputable breeders health test regardless of color.
What are champagne Labs?
Champagne is a lighter yellow, produced by a dilute gene on a yellow coat. It's a coat variation, not a separate breed.
Are Labs good apartment dogs?
They can be if you provide daily walks, play and training. Labs are large and energetic, so a small space works only with consistent exercise.
Do I need a fenced yard?
A fence isn't required, but it's very helpful for potty breaks and play. Without one, plan on leashed walks and supervised outdoor time.
Are Labs good with cats and other dogs?
Usually, yes, especially when introduced early and gradually. Supervise first meetings and give your cat vertical escape spaces.
Can a Lab be left alone while I work?
Adult Labs can manage a typical workday with exercise and a routine, but puppies cannot. Plan for midday potty breaks and companionship during the first months.
Do I need previous dog experience?
No. Labs are a good first breed because they're trainable and forgiving. Just be ready to commit to training and exercise.
How do I know what puppy will fit my family?
We watch temperament as the puppies grow and help you choose one that matches your household, whether calmer, bolder or somewhere in the middle.

Buying safely & avoiding scams

How do I avoid puppy scams?
Never send money to someone who won't video chat, show the parents or answer questions by phone. Be wary of very low prices, rushed payment and only-gift-card or wire requests. Read our puppy scam guide.
How can I verify a breeder is real?
Ask for a phone or video call, a live video of the puppies and parents, health-testing records and references. Look for a consistent online history and real reviews.
Why are some Lab puppies so cheap online?
Very low prices often mean no health testing, a scam or puppies from a high-volume source. Check what the price includes and ask for documentation.
What questions should I ask a breeder?
Ask about health testing, registration, temperament, how puppies are raised, what happens if you can't keep the dog and how long they've bred. Our list of 20 questions to ask can help.
Should I see the puppy's parents?
Yes, or at least see photos and videos of them, along with health records. Meeting the mother tells you a lot about your puppy's likely temperament.
What paperwork should come with a puppy?
AKC registration papers (or application), vaccine and deworming records, the puppy's health information and a contract. Ask what is included before you reserve.
Is it safe to pay a deposit to a breeder I haven't met?
Possibly, if you've verified them first. Speak by phone, ask for video, check reviews and use a traceable payment method. We're happy to answer any questions before you reserve.
What are red flags when buying a puppy?
Always-available puppies, multiple breeds, refusal to show the home or parents, no health records, pressure to pay fast and no contract are all warning signs.

Adult dogs, aging & lifestyle

How long do Labradors live?
Labs commonly live 10 to 12 years, and good weight, exercise and vet care can help them stay healthy longer. See our lifespan and senior care guide.
How big do English Labs get?
Most adults weigh roughly 55 to 85 pounds, with males usually larger. They are stockier than American Labs. Size guide.
When is a Lab fully grown?
Labs reach their adult height around a year, but keep filling out and maturing until about two years old.
How much exercise does an adult Lab need?
Typically an hour or more a day, split between walks, play and mental work. Puppy exercise guide.
How much exercise does a Lab puppy need?
Short, gentle sessions. A common rule of thumb is about five minutes of structured exercise per month of age, twice a day, plus free play and rest.
Do Labs like to swim?
Most do. Labs were bred as water retrievers and have a water-repellent coat and webbed feet. Introduce water gradually and never leave a dog unsupervised near a pool.
Can Labs do hunting or dock diving?
Yes. Labs excel in retrieving, field work, dock diving and many dog sports. Early training and good conditioning help.
Are Labs good therapy or service dogs?
Their temperament and trainability make them popular choices, though not every Lab is suited. Early socialization and structured training matter.
Do Labs get along with children?
Usually very well. Supervise young kids and puppies, and teach children gentle handling.
When do Labs get arthritis?
It's more common in senior years or in dogs with joint problems or excess weight. Keeping your Lab lean and active helps. Ask your vet about managing stiffness.
How do I care for a senior Lab?
Switch to regular vet checks, adjust diet and exercise, offer orthopedic bedding and non-slip floors and watch for changes in weight, mobility and appetite.
Should I get a second Lab?
Many families do. Wait until your first dog is trained and settled, and consider pairing a puppy with an adult of a different age or sex.
Do Labs need a lot of attention?
They are social dogs that thrive on time with their people. Daily play, training and companionship keep a Lab happy.
What are the best toys for Labs?
Durable rubber chew toys, rope toys, balls and puzzle feeders. Avoid small toys that can be swallowed, and replace worn ones.
